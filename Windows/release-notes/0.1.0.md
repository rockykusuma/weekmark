The first test build of Weekmark for Windows: the calendar week number, always visible on your desktop.
This is a pre-release for colleagues to try. The Mac app's download is unaffected.

## What it does

- **Desktop widget** with the same content and look as the Mac widget: the week number, date range and year ring;
  quarter, sprint and PI chips; the 7-day strip with holidays; the week's progress; up to 3 upcoming milestones;
  and a month grid with a CW column (hover a week for its details, click to pin it, scroll or ‹ › to change month).
- Sits **on the desktop behind your windows**, or floats above them. Drag it anywhere (it can be locked). It remembers
  its place per monitor. It has no taskbar button and stays out of Alt+Tab.
- **Tray icon showing the week number.** Its menu copies "CW43 (19–25 Oct)", shows or hides the widget, and opens the
  position, size and settings options.
- **Settings:** ISO or US week numbering, size, background, appearance, sprints, PIs, quarters, holidays for 10
  countries, your company's `.ics` file, and milestones.
- Starts with Windows (you can turn this off). It installs **without admin rights**, into your user profile.
- Its week numbers, holidays and working-day counts are checked against the Mac app's own answers.

## Which file to download

- **Setup:** `Weekmark-Windows-0.1.0-x64-Setup.exe` (most PCs) or `…-arm64-Setup.exe` (Snapdragon / ARM laptops).
  It installs per user and puts Weekmark in the Start menu.
- **Portable:** the `…-Portable.zip` files. Unzip anywhere and run `Weekmark.exe`. Nothing is installed.

Not sure which one you need? Open Settings → System → About. "System type" says x64 or ARM64.

## "Windows protected your PC"

This build is not code-signed yet (signing through the SignPath Foundation is under way), so SmartScreen may warn the
first time you run it. Click **More info**, then **Run anyway**. If your company laptop doesn't offer **Run anyway**, you
can [build Weekmark from source](https://github.com/rockykusuma/weekmark/blob/windows-v0.1.0/Windows/README.md) with
only the free .NET SDK. Copies you build yourself usually don't trigger the warning.
